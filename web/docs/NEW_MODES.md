# Mode and recording contracts

Escape Levels, Survival, Hunter and Challenges share flexible board geometry and local learning. Fruit moves teach the fruit policy; player-controlled snake moves teach the snake policy. Opponents combine learned predictions with legal routing.

Levels uses ten increasing score gates: 150, 250, 350, 450, 550, 650, 800, 950, 1100 and 1300. Each successful moved cell earns 1 / 1.5 / 2 points on Easy / Normal / Hard. Idle and blocked movement earns none. Reaching a target clears the map and unlocks the next; capture takes precedence. Snake length falls with score progress, and a crash respawns a hunter until the target is met. Unlocks persist separately from score history in the existing save; older stored wins migrate. Clearing score history keeps unlocks. Survival offers every map, scores 10 points/second times difficulty, retains snake length and respawns crashed hunters.

Hunter starts a four-cell player-controlled snake facing right. It moves at the selected difficulty cadence, buffers two perpendicular turns and rejects reversals. Fruit evades at 520/340/220 ms on Easy/Normal/Hard using learned choices and predicted human snake motion. Eating fruit adds one cell and 100 points times difficulty. Walls, stones and body collisions lose. The departing tail is traversable unless growing; filling all free cells wins. Seeded spawning enumerates free cells. Hunter offers all maps and has no fruit dash or close-call boost.

Canopy Sprint, Root Runner and Wild Escape retain fixed course IDs, arenas, seeds and 15/20/25-second objectives. Their board dimensions follow the viewport and opponents use current learned models. Capture takes precedence over the target. They are personal practice courses; a fixed seed does not promise identical adaptive opponents across devices. Results are stored separately by challenge ID and rules version.

One named Food character / Snake design option pair lives in Menu. Choices persist across rounds/routes/reloads until changed and freeze during active/paused play. Gameplay hitboxes remain cell-based. Touch swipes up/down/left/right set their matching direction; taps do not steer. Double-tap after a swipe dashes along the last direction in fruit-controlled modes. The three-cell dash, four-second cooldown and close-call boosts retain their collision/timing rules. Pause/restart clears continuing swipe movement. Keyboard controls remain available; on-screen arrows and Dash buttons are removed.

Replay format 2, version 1.3.0, ruleset `orchard-4`, captures geometry, appearance, initial neural snapshots and ordered steer, clear, dash, pause, resume, resize and fixed-step actions. Deterministic training reproduces evolving weights and final fingerprints. Consecutive identical steps are compressed. Retained engines support 1.2.0 / `orchard-3` / format 2 and 1.1.0 / `canopy-2` / format 1 with original terrain/rules. Unknown versions, malformed weights/dimensions/actions and oversized recordings are rejected. Three completed recordings are retained, each bounded to 250 KB, 20,000 action groups and 180,000 steps. Wall time, audio, render cadence and generated IDs/dates are outside playback guarantees.

Matching fruit-controlled ghosts use recorded full artwork at 32% opacity and require the same mode, map, difficulty, seed and dimensions. Live resize stops incompatible ghosts. Cosmetics/models can differ because the ghost represents its own recorded run; it never changes collisions, scoring or training.

Pause loops an independent cursor through saved gameplay, using the current prefix as a fallback, behind a blurred translucent overlay. The live round and regular ghost stay frozen. The bounded preview key may hold unscored/abandoned prefixes without creating score or completed-replay entries. Reduced motion keeps the preview static. Scores remain browser-local; cross-ruleset records are not a competitive comparison. Online PvP is described separately in ONLINE_PVP.md and is not implemented.
